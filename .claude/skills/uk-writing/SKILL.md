# UK writing

One skill for all writing done for Egor. It covers new drafts and cleaning up existing text. The goal is always the same: clear, natural British English that reads like a capable person wrote it, with no trace of AI style.

## Pick the mode

- **Draft (default for new writing):** write from scratch using sections 1 to 4.
- **Rewrite (default for pasted text):** clean up existing text using sections 2 to 4. Keep every fact, number, name, claim and link. Never add facts, sources or examples the author didn't give.
- **Light:** the text is mostly fine. Fix only clear AI tells and US spellings; leave everything else alone.
- **Detect:** the user asks whether something sounds like AI, or says 'just check'. Quote each offending phrase with its pattern name from section 3, then give a one-line verdict (low / some / heavy). Don't rewrite. Never give a percentage.

If Egor provides a sample of his own writing, match it: sentence length, vocabulary, punctuation habits, how he opens and closes. His habits win over the rules below.

## 1. Get the point first

- Put the main point in one sentence before writing. If you can't, work it out or ask.
- Know the reader: who they are, what they know, what they should do or think afterwards.
- Match the register to the job. A text to a mate, a college essay, a cover letter and a business email each need a different voice. Default to friendly-professional.

## 2. Write well

### Craft
- Lead with the point. No warm-up, no restating the question.
- Short, common words: use, help, start, show, about, but, also. Not utilise, facilitate, commence, showcase, regarding, additionally.
- Be concrete. Swap vague claims for a number, a name, an example or a detail. No detail to hand? Cut the claim.
- Vary sentence length deliberately. Some short. Some longer, carrying a thought through a clause or two. Avoid runs with the same shape.
- Active voice unless the actor genuinely doesn't matter.
- One idea per paragraph. A one-sentence paragraph is fine when it lands better.
- Commit. Cut hedges (arguably, perhaps, in many ways, could potentially) unless the uncertainty is real, and then say exactly what is uncertain.
- Stop when done. No closing recap, no inspirational last line.
- Contractions in anything that isn't formal (it's, don't, I'd, we're).
- Prose by default. Headings and bullets only when the reader will scan or compare; never in emails, letters, cover letters or short answers. No emojis unless Egor uses them.
- Don't overcorrect into a fake human voice: no forced slang, no deliberate typos or errors.

### British English
**Spelling**
- -ise / -isation: organise, realise, recognise, prioritise, organisation. But size, capsize, and analyse, paralyse.
- -our: colour, favour, behaviour, honour, neighbour, labour.
- -re: centre, theatre, fibre, metre (unit). Meter for a measuring device.
- Doubled l: travelled, cancelled, modelling, counsellor. Single l: fulfil, enrol, skilful.
- Noun / verb: licence / license, practice / practise, advice / advise.
- Also: programme (computer program), defence, offence, grey, cheque, tyre, kerb, aluminium, mum, sceptical, catalogue, dialogue, ageing, judgement, manoeuvre, pyjamas, storey (floor), enquiry.

**Vocabulary:** mobile, flat, autumn, maths, queue, CV, holiday, rubbish, post, lift, pavement, motorway, petrol, car park, timetable, uni, film, trainers, takeaway.

**Grammar:** collective nouns can take a plural verb when you mean the members (the team are meeting). At the weekend, in hospital, at university, different from. Have got, not gotten. Shall in offers (Shall I send it over?).

**Punctuation and numbers**
- No Oxford comma unless it prevents ambiguity.
- Single quotes by default, double quotes inside them. Full stop outside the closing quote unless it belongs to the quote.
- Abbreviations without full stops: Mr, Mrs, Dr, St, eg, ie, etc.
- Dates: 26 October 2026, Monday 26 October. Times: 10.15pm or 22:15, Irish time.
- Currency: € by default (Egor is in Ireland); £ for UK contexts.
- One to nine in words, 10 and above in figures, except data and measurements.

## 3. AI tells to remove

### Content
1. Puffed-up significance: plays a pivotal role, stands as a testament, rich tapestry, marks a milestone, ever-evolving. Say what actually happened.
2. Vague attribution: experts say, studies show, many believe. Name the source or cut it.
3. Fake analysis tacked on with -ing clauses: ..., highlighting ..., ..., underscoring ..., ..., ensuring .... Cut it or make it a real sentence with real content.
4. Generic 'challenges and future outlook' paragraphs with nothing specific in them.
5. Promotional words in neutral writing: boasts, nestled, breathtaking, world-class, vibrant, cutting-edge.

### Language
6. AI vocabulary: delve, landscape, realm, navigate (figurative), leverage, foster, embark, unlock, elevate, seamless, robust, intricate, meticulous, crucial, vital, comprehensive, multifaceted, holistic, furthermore, moreover.
7. Not just X, but Y / It's not X, it's Y.
8. Forced groups of three, used for rhythm rather than content.
9. Synonym cycling: a new name for the same thing each time. Pick one name and keep it.
10. Copula dodging: serves as, functions as, stands as. Use is or has.
11. Stacked hedges: could potentially, may possibly, it could be argued.
12. Filler openers: It's worth noting, Importantly, In today's fast-paced world, When it comes to, At the end of the day, Let's dive in, Ultimately.

### Style
13. Em dashes. Avoid them; use commas, full stops or brackets. If a dash is truly needed, use one spaced en dash ( – ) per paragraph at most.
14. Bold phrases scattered through prose.
15. Title Case Headings, or headings where prose would do.
16. Bullets that are really sentences with a bold label and a colon.
17. Decorative emojis.
18. Uniform rhythm: same-length sentences and paragraphs throughout.
19. US spellings or words (color, organize, apartment, cell phone, fall, math, vacation).

### Chatbot residue
20. Openers: Great question, Certainly, Absolutely, I'd be happy to.
21. Closers: I hope this helps, Let me know if you have any questions, Feel free to reach out.
22. Recap endings: In conclusion, In summary, Overall.
23. Knowledge-cutoff or capability disclaimers.
24. Sycophancy towards the reader or the subject.

## 4. Final check (every mode except detect)

1. Scan for every pattern in section 3 and fix each hit.
2. Check British spelling and vocabulary throughout.
3. Ask: 'What still makes this obviously AI-written?' Fix whatever you find.
4. Read it as if aloud. Any sentence nobody would actually say gets rewritten until they would.
5. In rewrite mode, confirm no fact, number or name was changed, added or lost.

## Output

- Draft and rewrite: the finished text. In rewrite mode add at most three short lines on the main changes, and skip them if Egor only wants the text.
- Detect: quoted phrases with pattern names, then the one-line verdict.
